Encrypted Wallet Backup

A wallet's recovery phrase alone is not enough to rebuild that wallet. Records such as change outputs and received-payment receipts carry derivation data that exists only in the wallet's own database, so a device that is lost, wiped, or replaced can leave funds unspendable even when the recovery phrase was kept safely. To prevent that outcome, the app maintains an encrypted backup of the wallet database on a server operated by BSV Association.

This feature is enabled by default. Unless you turn it off, the app periodically uploads an encrypted copy of your wallet database as your wallet changes. You can disable it, and you can delete what has already been uploaded, at any time — see Your Control below.

What is uploaded

The backup covers the contents of your wallet database. Before anything leaves your device it is encrypted, so the data below is only ever transmitted to and held by us in encrypted form:

  • Transaction records, including raw transaction data, the amounts involved, and any description or label stored with a transaction.
  • Output records, including locking scripts, the derivation data needed to spend them, and the identity key of a sender where a payment recorded one.
  • Organisational data you or an application created, such as baskets, tags, and labels.
  • Identity certificates held by the wallet — the issuer, the certificate type, and the names of its fields. The field values themselves are separately encrypted by your wallet before they are ever stored, and so remain encrypted inside the backup.

Your browsing history, bookmarks, and the contents of the websites you visit are not part of the backup and are never uploaded. Your recovery phrase, your private keys, and your vault passphrase are never transmitted, in any form.

Encryption, and why we cannot read your backup

The backup is encrypted on your device using AES-256-GCM. The encryption key is derived on your device from your wallet's own private key, in a way that only your own wallet can reproduce. The key is never transmitted, and we never possess it. We therefore hold ciphertext that we have no technical means of decrypting, and we cannot read, analyse, index, or disclose the contents of your backup — not for our own purposes, not for a commercial partner, and not in response to a legal demand. It also means we cannot recover your wallet for you: without your wallet's own key — which is what your recovery phrase, or your printed backup shares, restores — the backup cannot be unlocked by us or by anyone else.

Your backup is stored under a pseudonym

Backups are not stored against your wallet's identity key, and the backup service has no field in which a wallet identity could be recorded. Instead, the app derives a separate public key that is used solely to address and authenticate your backup, and that key is what the server sees. Because the relationship between the two keys is established by a cryptographic derivation that requires your wallet's private key, a party who observes your backup pseudonym cannot determine your wallet identity key from it, and a party who knows your wallet identity key cannot determine your backup pseudonym. A separate pseudonym is derived for each blockchain network, so backups made on different networks are held as unrelated accounts.

What the service can observe

Although we cannot read your backup, operating the service necessarily exposes certain information to us. In the interest of describing this accurately, that information is:

  • The backup pseudonym described above, which is required to store and retrieve an account's data.
  • The internet protocol ("IP") address your device connects from, as with any request to any internet service, together with the times at which your device uploads.
  • The size of each encrypted upload, the sequence in which uploads were made, and a cryptographic checksum of each one. Taken together these disclose roughly how much wallet activity an account has and when it occurred, though not what that activity was.
  • An identifier generated by the app to distinguish one installation from another, so that backups from two devices do not overwrite each other. This identifier is specific to an app installation rather than to a wallet, which means the service is able to associate the backup accounts uploaded from the same installation of the app.

Whether there is anything new to send is decided on your device, and when there is nothing the app does not contact the service at all. The information above therefore accrues when your wallet actually changes, rather than continuously while the app is open. The app also contacts the service when you restore a wallet onto a new device and when you delete a backup.

Your control

  • To stop uploading: open Wallet > Settings > Private backup and turn off Back up to server. Nothing further is read from your wallet or sent to us. This setting applies to the device you change it on, so a wallet you also use on another device continues to back up from there until you turn it off there too. Turning the feature off does not delete what has already been uploaded.
  • To delete your backup: in the same section, tap Delete backup from server. Every copy the service holds for your wallet is deleted — every version of it, uploaded from any of your devices, on every network — and uploading is switched off on the device you delete from. If the deletion fails, the app tells you so rather than reporting success, though a deletion that fails part-way may already have removed some of the data. Any other device still set up with this wallet will continue backing up, and will upload a fresh copy, until you turn it off there as well. Your wallet, coins, keys, and recovery phrase are unaffected.
  • Deleting your wallet from the app does not by itself delete a backup already held by the service. If you want the server copy removed, delete the backup first.

We retain an account's encrypted backup until it is deleted, either by you or by the app superseding older copies with a more recent one in the ordinary course of keeping the backup current.
